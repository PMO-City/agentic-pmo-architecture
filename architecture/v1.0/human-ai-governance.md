# Human–AI governance

## The boundary in one sentence

AI may extend organizational capability, but it does not become organizational authority merely because it can generate content, call tools, or act quickly.

## Five distinctions

The architecture keeps these five ideas separate:

| Concept | Question |
| --- | --- |
| Capability | What can this participant or system technically do? |
| Permission | What operation is it allowed to attempt? |
| Authority | Who or what has legitimate organizational standing to direct or approve it? |
| Execution | What action was actually performed, by whom or what, and when? |
| Accountability | Who remains answerable for the consequence and the decision? |

Collapsing these distinctions is one of the fastest ways to create unsafe automation. A model may be capable of sending a message, have a technical permission to call a service, lack business authority to make the commitment, and still require a human accountable for the result.

## What governed AI contributes

Governed AI may contribute to:

- preparation and synthesis;
- monitoring and attention detection;
- coordination and follow-up;
- explanation and comparison;
- bounded execution under explicit authorization;
- identification of uncertainty, conflict, or missing evidence; and
- maintenance of consistent representations across systems.

These are capabilities, not an unconditional grant of authority.

## What remains human or institutional

Humans or accountable institutions remain responsible for:

- legitimate organizational intent;
- consequential judgment;
- ethical and contextual interpretation;
- acceptance of material Evidence;
- approval or rejection of consequential actions;
- resolution of ambiguity and conflict;
- suspension or termination of governed activity; and
- accountability for decisions and outcomes.

This does not prevent automation. It defines the conditions under which automation remains governable.

## AI Assignment

An **AI Assignment** describes the governed work an AI participant is permitted to support. It should identify purpose, scope, applicable Activity or Project, allowed operations, prohibited operations, relevant context, Evidence obligations, intervention conditions, and accountability boundaries.

An assignment should be current. A permission that was valid earlier must not be assumed valid after the mandate, Activity, participant, policy, or operating condition changes.

## Acting and represented identity

When AI participates in work, the implementation should preserve:

- the acting technical principal or mechanism;
- the represented human, role, or institution where applicable;
- the governing Activity or Assignment;
- the authority and authorization context;
- the Evidence produced; and
- the accountable owner.

“The agent did it” is not sufficient attribution for consequential work.

## Consequence and oversight

Oversight should be proportionate to consequence. A low-risk draft may be generated automatically and reviewed later. A consequential external action may require prior human approval, stronger Evidence, dual review, or an explicit ability to suspend execution.

The important question is not whether a human clicked every button. It is whether the applicable human or institutional judgment, authority, and accountability were preserved for the consequence at hand.

## Explainability

Explainability should let an authorized reviewer understand, to the degree appropriate for the consequence:

- what the AI was asked to do;
- which context and sources it used;
- what Evidence supported its output;
- what assumptions and uncertainty were present;
- which assignment and authority boundaries applied;
- what action it proposed or performed; and
- what happened afterward.

An explanation is not merely a natural-language justification generated after the fact. It should remain connected to the source, time, context, and governing Activity.

## Intervention and safe degradation

Governed AI should support human intervention. An authorized human should be able to pause, redirect, reject, correct, or terminate material work within the applicable responsibility boundary.

When context, authorization, Evidence, or service reliability is insufficient, the safe response may be to stop, ask for judgment, preserve the unresolved condition, or degrade to a less autonomous mode. Continuing silently is not always the most reliable behavior.

## Integration boundary

External systems may remain authoritative for the records they own. Integration should preserve source ownership, identity, authority, Evidence, version, time, reconciliation, and failure state.

Connecting a system to an AI participant does not transfer business authority to the connector, the model, or the workflow engine.

## Review questions

- Is the AI's capability distinct from its permission?
- Is permission distinct from legitimate authority?
- Is the accountable human or institution explicit?
- Can the implementation reconstruct what the AI saw and did?
- Are current rights revalidated before consequential execution?
- Can an authorized person intervene?
- Does failure preserve uncertainty rather than fabricate completion?
