# The Agentic PMO operating model

## A continuous loop, not a reporting cycle

The Agentic PMO connects nine stages of organizational operation. They are shown as a loop because learning and durable knowledge influence future intent and future work.

```text
Organizational intent
        ↓
Governed Activities and Projects
        ↓
Human and AI execution
        ↓
Evidence and operational change
        ↓
Living Governed State
        ↓
Workspace Projections and Decision Landscapes
        ↓
Human judgment and governed follow-up
        ↓
Validated organizational learning
        ↓
Digital DNA
        ↺ informs future organizational intent
```

The arrows describe semantic responsibility, not a mandatory software sequence. An implementation may use queues, workflows, event streams, databases, documents, or human processes. The technology may vary; the meaning and governance boundaries must remain visible.

## 1. Organizational intent

Intent is the reason governed work exists. It may be expressed through a mandate, objective, decision, commitment, policy, obligation, or other legitimate organizational direction.

Intent should be attributable and scoped. It should be possible to ask who or what authorized the direction, what it applies to, when it is effective, and what constraints or exclusions matter.

Intent is not the same as a vague wish, an unreviewed suggestion, or an AI-generated priority.

## 2. Governed Activities and Projects

An Activity is the fundamental governed unit of operational work. A Project is a governed commitment that coordinates Activities toward intended outcomes.

Not every Activity belongs to a Project. A compliance response, incident investigation, decision preparation task, or organizational learning activity may be governed without being part of a traditional project plan.

Each governed Activity should make its purpose, owner, accountability, authority context, completion conditions, evidence obligations, and applicable method visible.

## 3. Human and AI execution

Execution may be performed by a person, an AI participant, an enterprise system, a partner, or a real-world actor. The location of execution does not change the Activity's meaning or remove its governance obligations.

AI participation requires more than capability. The implementation should preserve the applicable assignment, current eligibility, technical authorization, business authority, represented participant, and human oversight conditions. See [Human–AI governance](human-ai-governance.md).

## 4. Evidence and operational change

Execution produces Evidence and may change the operating environment. Evidence should identify its source, context, time, subject, relationship to the Activity, uncertainty, and challenge or correction status where material.

An output is not automatically Evidence, and Evidence is not automatically accepted truth. Acceptance belongs to the responsible domain or authority.

## 5. Living Governed State

The Living Governed State is the current, governed representation of organizational reality within a declared scope. It is not identical to a report, dashboard, database, or model context window.

It should distinguish current accepted state from proposed, inferred, historical, stale, forecast, superseded, rejected, and scenario-bound material.

## 6. Workspace Projections and Decision Landscapes

A Workspace Projection is a purpose-specific, access-aware representation of the Living Governed State for a participant or system. Different participants may see different projections without receiving different organizational truths.

A Decision Landscape organizes the relevant state, evidence, uncertainty, dependencies, options, risks, authority, and learning for a human decision. It prepares judgment; it does not replace it.

## 7. Human judgment and governed follow-up

Humans decide when consequence, ambiguity, accountability, ethics, or trust requires judgment. They may approve, reject, redirect, suspend, terminate, correct, or request more evidence.

Follow-up should remain governed. A reminder, escalation, recommendation, or AI-generated command is not automatically a decision or an authorized action.

## 8. Validated organizational learning

Experience becomes learning only after it is assessed for context, evidence, applicability, uncertainty, and possible counterexamples. A successful outcome may suggest a learning candidate; it does not prove that a method will work everywhere.

Learning should remain attributable and challengeable. It should be possible to distinguish a proposal from validated organizational knowledge.

## 9. Digital DNA

Digital DNA is durable, validated organizational knowledge that can inform future operation. It is not raw history and not generic AI memory.

Digital DNA should preserve its source, validation basis, scope, applicability, version, uncertainty, and conditions for reassessment. It can influence future intent and methods without becoming invisible or permanent authority.

## Two loops that should not be confused

The nine-step loop describes the operating model of the organization. An implementation may also use a smaller Activity acceptance loop:

```text
Trigger
  → Activity and method binding
  → authority and technical authorization
  → execution
  → evidence
  → reconciliation and acceptance
  → disposition
  → outcome or explicit outcome disposition
```

The smaller loop governs whether a particular Activity is established, executed, reconciled, and closed. It is part of the larger operating model; it does not replace it.

## The loop's test

When reviewing an implementation, ask:

- Can the work be traced to legitimate intent?
- Is the work bounded as an Activity or Project?
- Are human and AI responsibilities explicit?
- Is execution attributable?
- Is Evidence separated from assumptions and proposals?
- Is current state separated from projections and scenarios?
- Can a human intervene before consequential action?
- Is learning validated before reuse?
- Can future users inspect why the knowledge exists?
