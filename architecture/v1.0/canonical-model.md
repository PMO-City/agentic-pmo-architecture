# Canonical model

The Agentic PMO uses a small set of stable concepts to keep meaning consistent across people, AI participants, enterprise systems, workflows, and representations. A product may use different names internally, but it should be able to map its meaning to these concepts without silently changing their boundaries.

## Intent and legitimacy

**Organizational intent** is the direction that gives governed work a reason to exist. A **Mandate** is a scoped, attributable, time-aware expression of legitimate authority, obligation, permission, or purpose. Intent can also be expressed through objectives, commitments, policies, or decisions, but it should remain possible to identify the source and applicability of the direction.

## Activity

An **Activity** is the fundamental governed unit of operational work. It connects purpose, owner, accountability, authority, participants, method, execution, evidence, completion, and outcome.

An Activity may be performed by a human, AI participant, enterprise system, partner, or real-world actor. It is not merely a task record, prompt, message, or workflow instance.

## Project

A **Project** is a governed organizational commitment that coordinates Activities toward one or more intended outcomes for a defined purpose.

A Project supplies context, governance, accountability, and coordination. Activities perform the work. A Project can contain Activities without owning every semantic responsibility of those Activities.

## Execution

**Execution** is the performance of governed work. It may include human judgment, AI preparation, system transactions, external collaboration, or physical activity.

Execution capability does not by itself establish business authority. A technical action can be successful while still being unauthorized, incorrectly attributed, or insufficiently evidenced.

## Evidence

**Evidence** is attributable material that supports understanding, reconciliation, acceptance, explanation, or challenge. Evidence should be considered in context and time.

Evidence can be reported, inferred, proposed, verified, accepted, rejected, superseded, disputed, stale, or unavailable. A generated output may contribute to Evidence without automatically becoming accepted state.

## Living Governed State

The **Living Governed State** is the maintained representation of current organizational reality within a declared boundary.

It preserves the distinction between:

| State | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Current accepted | The best currently accepted representation within scope. |
| Proposed | A candidate future or changed state awaiting decision or acceptance. |
| Historical | A prior state retained for lineage and reconstruction. |
| Scenario or forecast | A possible or expected state used for analysis. |
| Stale or uncertain | Material whose freshness, confidence, or applicability is limited. |
| Rejected or superseded | Material that must not be presented as current accepted state. |

## Workspace Projection

A **Workspace Projection** is a purpose-specific, access-aware representation of exactly one declared Living Governed State for an authorized participant, AI assignment, or system.

Projection changes what a participant can see, not what the organization means. It should preserve source identity, time, restrictions, uncertainty, and provenance where material.

## Decision Landscape

A **Decision Landscape** organizes current state, Evidence, dependencies, risks, options, uncertainty, authority, and relevant learning for human judgment.

It is a prepared decision context, not a decision authority. AI may assemble or explain it; the accountable human or institution remains responsible for the consequential decision.

## Method and binding

A **PMO Method** is a governed way of performing a class of work. A **Method Application Binding** records the exact applicable method and version for a particular Activity or Project.

A prompt, template, workflow, or model output may help realize a method. It does not automatically become the method or establish that a method was authorized and applied.

## Outcome, Benefit, Value, and Retained Value

These concepts should remain distinct:

- **Output:** what execution produced.
- **Outcome:** what changed as a result of the work.
- **Benefit:** a favorable effect for an identified beneficiary or purpose.
- **Value:** an assessed significance of effects relative to purpose, stakeholders, resources, alternatives, constraints, risk, and time.
- **Retained Value:** value that remains available or useful over time under stated conditions.

Completion does not prove outcome. Outcome does not prove benefit. Benefit does not prove value. Initial value does not prove retained value.

## Attention Need and Decision Need

An **Attention Need** identifies why governed attention may be required. A **Decision Need** identifies why human or institutional judgment may be required.

A signal, alert, model output, or stakeholder request can contribute evidence for an Attention Need or Decision Need. It does not independently establish priority, authority, commitment, or decision outcome.

## Learning and Digital DNA

A **Learning Candidate** is a bounded proposition about what execution may have taught the organization. It remains subject to evidence, context, challenge, and validation.

**Validated organizational learning** is learning accepted for a declared scope and purpose. **Digital DNA** is durable, versioned organizational knowledge derived from validated learning and retained with its provenance and conditions of use.

## Heartbeat

A **Heartbeat** continuously observes governed conditions and may initiate coordination, reminders, reconciliation, escalation, or attention work.

Detection does not imply authority to act. A Heartbeat can identify a condition while leaving decision and execution with the appropriate human or governed participant.

## One definition, many representations

The same concept may appear in a database, event, document, dashboard, AI context, or external system. Representation is not semantic ownership.

An implementation should be able to answer:

- Which concept is being represented?
- Who owns its meaning and accepted state?
- Which source is authoritative?
- What version and time apply?
- Which participant created or changed it?
- What Evidence supports it?
- What authority permits the operation?
